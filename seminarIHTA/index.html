The Complex Image Method for Urban Scenarios

Seminar Project at the Institute for Hearing Technology and Acoustics | RWTH Aachen University

Project Overview

This project investigates ground effects in outdoor sound propagation and how they can be modeled more accurately in urban and outdoor environments. Classical image-source approaches based on plane-wave reflection often fail to explain real-world phenomena in scenarios where sources and receivers are close to the ground and ground impedance plays a significant role. Measurements outdoors showed surprising effects such as shifted interference minima, low-frequency amplification, and broad attenuation dips observed over porous ground surfaces.

In this work, the Complex Image Method is investigated as an advanced model for sound reflection at finite-impedance ground surfaces. The underlying theory of spherical wave propagation in outdoor environments is derived and compared to the classical plane-wave reflection model. The Complex Image Method provides the best agreement with measured data, especially for low source/receiver heights and shallow grazing angles.

The work was conducted as part of a seminar project at the Institute for Hearing Technology and Acoustics at RWTH Aachen University supervised Pascal Palenda, M.Sc. and Prof. Dr. Michael Vorländer.
